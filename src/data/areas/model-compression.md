Model compression focuses on reducing the computational and memory footprint of large language models while preserving their performance. As LLMs scale into the billions of parameters, compression has become a critical research direction for enabling efficient deployment across diverse hardware settings—from cloud clusters to edge and mobile devices. Techniques such as quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, and low-rank adaptation aim to identify and retain the most informative components of a model while removing redundancy introduced during large-scale training. Recent advances also explore task-aware and dynamic compression methods, including KV cache compression for inference and adaptive routing schemes that selectively activate model submodules. Together, these approaches provide a principled framework for balancing accuracy with efficiency, allowing researchers to build models that are faster, smaller, and more cost-effective without sacrificing capability. As demand grows for real-time, energy-efficient, and privacy-preserving AI systems, model compression continues to play a foundational role in bridging frontier-model performance with practical deployment constraints across the AI ecosystem.
